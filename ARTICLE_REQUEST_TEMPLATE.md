# Median Point Article Request Form v1

Use this form whenever you want a Median Point article package from research material. You can fill every field, or leave non-essential ones blank.

The goal is to give enough context for proper template selection before drafting begins.

## Field Guide

### `Working title`

A rough working headline. It does not need to be final.

### `Core question`

The single most important field.

This should be the real editorial question the piece must answer. Good examples:

- `How has India's position in the Indo-Pacific changed since 2021, and what explains the shift?`
- `Which explanation best accounts for the climate change debate in this case?`
- `What does the latest trade data actually show, and what does it not show?`

Avoid vague prompts like:

- `Write about BRICS`
- `Make an article on semiconductors`
- `Explain this issue`

### `Article type if known`

Optional. If you already know the intended format, say so. If not, leave it blank and let the system choose.

Accepted starting labels:

- `Strategic Affairs Analysis`
- `Synthesized Opinion`
- `Explainer`
- `Investigation`
- `Data Story`

### `Topic summary`

A short summary of what the article is about and why it matters.

2-5 sentences is enough.

### `Time window`

Important for any article where chronology, historical comparison, or archive-safe writing matters.

Examples:

- `2021-2026`
- `Post-October 2023`
- `Cold War to present`

### `Primary region or subject`

The geographic or conceptual center of the article.

Examples:

- `Indo-Pacific`
- `India-US trade`
- `Climate policy`
- `Rare earth supply chains`

### `Main actors or subjects`

List the states, institutions, firms, theories, datasets, people, or concepts that actually matter.

Examples:

- `India, United States, China, Japan, ASEAN`
- `Theory A, Theory B, Theory C`
- `Lowy Asia Power Index, defense spending, trade data`

### `Your angle or thesis`

Optional but very useful.

This is where you say what you suspect, what you want pressure-tested, or what you think the central argument may be.

Examples:

- `I think India's leverage is growing, but mainly through strategic optionality rather than military superiority.`
- `I lean toward the structural explanation, but I want the ideological explanation tested fairly.`

### `Target reader`

Who is this for?

Examples:

- `General but informed reader`
- `India-focused policy reader`
- `Geopolitics audience with limited technical background`

### `Desired length`

Examples:

- `1200-1600 words`
- `1800-2500 words`
- `3000+ words`

### `Target category`

Use the current site categories where possible:

- `Geopolitics`
- `Defense & Security`
- `Economy & Trade`
- `Diplomacy`
- `Analysis`
- `Opinion`

### `Research material`

This is where you provide the raw material.

It can include:

- your research document
- your notes
- links
- source extracts
- PDFs
- screenshots
- rough draft text
- timelines
- spreadsheets or numeric tables

This field can be short if files are already attached or present in the repo.

### `Must include`

List anything that cannot be omitted.

Examples:

- specific documents
- a key event
- a quote
- a chart
- a case study
- a legal caution

### `Must avoid`

This is very important for quality control.

Use it for:

- weak claims
- uncertain allegations
- loaded framing
- overstatement
- unsupported accusations
- legal risk areas

### `Known visuals or embeds`

List any asset you already have or know you want to use.

Examples:

- `Map of maritime chokepoints`
- `Tweet from minister`
- `YouTube speech clip`
- `Before/after image pair`
- `Datawrapper chart`

### `Related Median Point articles`

Optional internal linking context.

Useful when:

- a previous article should be referenced
- the new article is part of a series
- a related explainer already exists

### `Desired output level`

Choose one of these:

- `Blueprint only`
- `Blueprint + CMS plan`
- `Full draft`
- `Full publication package`

Use `Full publication package` when you want site-ready output with frontmatter, CMS placement guidance, FAQ, and references.

## Recommended Minimal Version

If you want to move fast, this shorter form is enough for many cases.

```txt
MEDIAN POINT ARTICLE REQUEST

Working title:
Core question:
Topic summary:
Research material:
Must include:
Must avoid:
Desired output level:
```

## What The System Should Return

A complete request should usually produce:

1. `Chosen template`
2. `Why that template fits`
3. `Refined editorial question`
4. `Planning dossier`
5. `Narrative blueprint`
6. `CMS blueprint`
7. `Draft article`
8. `FAQ and references`
9. `Frontmatter recommendation`
10. `Optional visual placeholders`
11. `QA notes`

## Example Filled Form

```txt
MEDIAN POINT ARTICLE REQUEST

Working title:
How India's leverage in the Indo-Pacific is changing

Core question:
How has India's position in the Indo-Pacific changed since 2021, and what explains that change?

Article type if known:
Strategic Affairs Analysis

Topic summary:
This piece examines whether India is becoming more strategically important in the Indo-Pacific and whether that change is primarily military, economic, diplomatic, or geographic. The article should explain why the shift matters now.

Time window:
2021-2026

Primary region or subject:
Indo-Pacific

Main actors or subjects:
India, United States, China, Japan, ASEAN

Your angle or thesis:
India is gaining leverage, but mainly through strategic optionality, geography, and supply-chain positioning rather than raw military dominance.

Target reader:
General but informed geopolitical reader

Desired length:
2200-2800 words

Target category:
Geopolitics

Research material:
Attached research memo, notes, and source links.

Must include:
Lowy Asia Power Index, supply-chain realignment, Taiwan Strait relevance, Indian Ocean geography

Must avoid:
Overstating military superiority, vague alliance language, unsupported predictions

Known visuals or embeds:
Map of the first island chain, shipping route map, one tweet from an official source

Related Median Point articles:
the-balance-of-power-in-the-indo-pacific

Desired output level:
Full publication package
```

## Editorial Rule

The better the `core question`, `research material`, and `must avoid` fields are, the better the resulting article package will be.
