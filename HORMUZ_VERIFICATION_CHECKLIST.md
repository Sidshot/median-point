# The Hormuz Ultimatum - Verification Checklist v1

This checklist exists to protect the flagship draft from drifting beyond what the current source pack can safely support.

Use it before moving the article from working draft to publication-ready draft.

## Current Verified Baseline

The following points have now been checked directly against the local source pack and can be treated as the stronger floor of the working draft:

- The Islamabad Memorandum is being treated as a 14-point framework signed on 17 June 2026.
- The current working wording that it ended strikes, reopened Hormuz toll-free for 60 days, ended the U.S. naval blockade, and provided oil-related sanctions waivers is grounded in the local MoU source cluster.
- Operation Urja Suraksha is being treated as launching on 23 March 2026 to protect energy supplies and sea lines of communication.
- The working draft's use of Praying Mantis as a doctrinal hinge is supported by direct source-pack material showing the scale and punitive character of the 18 April 1988 U.S. operation.
- The current draft can safely state that Hormuz carries roughly one-fifth of global LNG and about one-quarter of seaborne oil trade in the source pack's 2023-2025 framing.

These checks strengthen the draft, but they do not remove the need for careful phrasing in the high-risk zones below.

## 1. Highest-Risk Claim Zones

These are the parts of the article most likely to require additional verification or tighter wording.

### A. Legal Regime of Hormuz

Check:

- how transit passage is being described
- how innocent passage is being described
- whether Iran's treaty position is stated precisely
- whether Oman's role is described accurately
- whether any claim about tolling, permits, or management authority is directly backed by a strong source

Watch for:

- overstating legal certainty
- presenting one wartime interpretation as settled law
- implying a fully institutionalized toll regime if the evidence is thinner than that

### B. Functional Closure Claims

Check:

- whether the draft distinguishes physical closure from economic closure
- whether traffic collapse claims are sourced tightly
- whether insurance-market and shipping behavior claims are backed by something more than broad commentary

Watch for:

- using "closed" too casually
- implying a total halt if the evidence only supports severe disruption

### C. Bypass Capacity Claims

Check:

- each named pipeline system
- stated versus usable capacity
- whether the total bypass figure is framed as approximate rather than falsely precise
- whether the article clearly says bypasses mitigate but do not replace Hormuz

Watch for:

- treating nominal engineering capacity as actual crisis throughput
- overconfident percentages without clear source basis

### D. War Aims versus Settlement Terms

Check:

- exact public rationales attributed to the United States and Israel
- exact settlement terms attributed to the Islamabad framework
- whether sanctions and reconstruction language is presented accurately

Watch for:

- overclaiming "capitulation"
- using stronger diplomatic language than the MoU actually supports

### E. Civilian Casualty and Targeting Claims

Check:

- whether casualty figures are clearly attributed
- whether forensic claims are described as verified, contested, or likely
- whether any allegation of intentional targeting goes beyond the evidence

Watch for:

- using contested casualty numbers as settled fact
- conflating munition identification with proven targeting intent

### F. Deterrence Failure Argument

Check:

- whether the argument is grounded in base exposure, retaliatory logic, and alliance stress
- whether the draft avoids vague "decline" or civilizational language

Watch for:

- turning a structural argument into a rhetorical flourish
- claiming the Gulf order collapsed when the evidence supports weakening or exposure more clearly

### G. India Implications

Check:

- energy exposure claims
- operational details around Operation Urja Suraksha
- whether the section stays linked to Hormuz rather than wandering into broad India doctrine

Watch for:

- overextending the India angle
- treating one operation as proof of a total strategic transformation

## 2. Required Distinctions

These distinctions must remain visible in the final piece.

### `Documented`

Use direct language for:

- event dates
- memorandum signing
- operation names
- chokepoint relevance
- stated throughput figures when properly sourced

### `Inferred`

Use analytical language for:

- deterrence failure
- coercive usability of Hormuz
- alliance-architecture stress
- strategic autonomy behavior

Preferred phrasing:

- `the evidence suggests`
- `the crisis appears to show`
- `a stronger inference is`
- `the most defensible conclusion is`

### `Unresolved`

Use explicit uncertainty language for:

- internal war aims
- long-term order change
- full legal durability of wartime claims
- ultimate diplomatic significance of the MoU

Preferred phrasing:

- `it is too early to conclude`
- `the available material does not establish`
- `this remains contested`
- `stronger primary evidence would be needed`

## 3. Section-Specific Verification Priorities

### `The Long Fuse`

Verify:

- historical compression is accurate
- no section turns into a generic Iran history
- the doctrinal hinge from Praying Mantis to asymmetry is expressed carefully

### `How the System Was Built to Fail`

Verify:

- GCC and U.S. basing statements are proportionate
- proxy-network discussion does not overstate coordination where evidence is mixed

### `Why Hormuz Matters More Than Maps Suggest`

Verify:

- throughput figures
- Asian destination share
- legal ambiguity wording

### `From Threat to Mechanism`

Verify:

- all language around tolling, management, and legal reinterpretation
- economic closure logic
- commercial behavior claims

### `The Diplomatic Reversal`

Verify:

- sanctions relief phrasing
- reconstruction amount
- what the agreement explicitly says versus what commentators infer from it

### `What This Meant for India`

Verify:

- operational description of Urja Suraksha
- energy dependence figures
- strategic implications kept proportionate

## 4. Recommended Source Upgrade Path

Before final publication, try to tighten the draft using stronger source classes where possible.

Best upgrades:

- primary treaty text
- official memorandum text
- official military statements
- institutional energy datasets
- reputable outlet forensic reporting for contested strikes
- direct legal analysis from strong institutional or scholarly sources

Use weaker source classes only where:

- they are clearly identified as summary material
- they are not carrying the article's most sensitive claims alone

## 5. Final Pre-Publish Questions

Ask these before treating the draft as publication-ready:

- Is Hormuz still the center of the article?
- Is any strong claim resting on only one weak summary source?
- Are legal claims tighter than the rhetoric around them?
- Are the most dramatic assertions still the most defensible ones?
- Has the draft confused disruption with total closure anywhere?
- Has the article named what remains unknown?
- Does the conclusion stay inside what the evidence can actually bear?

If any answer is "no," revise before publishing.
