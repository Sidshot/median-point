> There is something inherently **diabolical** about a man who can shed his political skin as easily as a snake, only to emerge more deadlier than before. The transformation of Himanta Biswa Sarma—from an ardent, hardcore left-wing Congressi to the poster child of right-wing BJP —isn't just a change of heart; it's a **meticulously engineered masterclass in opportunism.**&#x20;



While I write this piece at late night in my room the political accusations on Sarma has been quite harsh. The latest storm to hit this fortress of power comes from Pawan Khera, who recently threw a bucket of mud directly at the Chief Minister’s doorstep, alleging a global web of hidden assets. The accusations are wild—multiple foreign passports from the UAE to Antigua, luxury properties in Dubai, and secret investments in Wyoming. It’s the kind of high-stakes scandal that would bury a lesser politician, but Sarma’s response was characteristically aggressive. He didn't just deny it; he weaponized the attack, claiming the documents were "Photoshopped" fakes sourced from Pakistani social media to rig the upcoming elections. By filing FIRs and threatening to drag the top Congress leadership into the legal mire, he turned a corruption allegation into a battle of national security and "foreign-influenced misinformation." It’s a classic Himanta move: don’t just defend the goal, burn down the other team's stadium.



&#x20;Allow me to give you a bit of his past whihc also is quite a journey. His career didn’t start in a plush office; it began in the cut-throat trenches of student politics at Cotton College, where he learned early on that loyalty is a currency meant to be spent, not saved. He cut his teeth with the AASU, only to turn around and dismantle his own mentors, like Bhrigu Phukan, the moment a better offer from the Congress came along. For over a decade, he was the ultimate "fixer" for Tarun Gogoi, building a resume of ruthless efficiency—medical colleges, teacher appointments, and infrastructure—while quietly constructing a parallel power structure that eventually outgrew the man who gave him a seat at the table. When the Congress "family" business refused to let him sit at the head, he didn’t just leave; he burned the bridge and built a highway to the BJP. He traded the white khadi for the saffron scarf not out of sudden conviction, but because he knew the throne he wanted was no longer in the hands of the old guard. From a campus agitator to the undisputed kingmaker of the Northeast, his career is a trail of outsmarted allies and discarded ideologies, all leading to the one thing he actually believes in: absolute control. Talk about Machiavelli as Sarma's journey seems as if he read and followed "The Prince" word by word.



### Sniffing the Wind

> This wasn't a sudden epiphany or a spiritual awakening. This was a man sniffing the direction of the wind and realizing his boat was on a sinking Congress ship. He didn't just jump overboard; he steered himself directly into the path of the winning side. This level of calculation makes him a **dangerous and unpredictable** force. For a politician like Sarma, ideology is a secondary tool—a costume to be swapped out when the audience changes. His only true north is his own **vested interest.** Sarma’s real "talent" lies in his ability to prey on the laymen and the ignorant. He doesn't just govern; he **milks the support** of voters by fueling hatred and keeping the fires of social friction burning. Whether it's the "Mama" persona or the sharp, witty barbs he throws at the opposition, every word is weighed for its ability to manipulate. He’s been in the game since his college days; he knows the gritty reality of the streets, and he knows exactly what to say to whom to get things done his way. Ultimately, we are looking at a man for whom the cost of power is never too high. He is **loathsome** to those who value consistency, but he is undeniably effective at staying relevant. By projecting himself as the "protector" of identity while simultaneously weaponizing fear, he has built a fortress of support that feels less like a democratic mandate and more like a captured audience.



**The Conclusion is simple:** The Congress party considers him 'Gaddar' and BJP and its ardent hardcore supporters see him as someone who is back to his home. Psychologically, Sarma is a high-functioning survivalist who treats politics like a blood sport played with a grandmaster’s precision. He possesses a "PhD in power"—not just the literal degree on his wall, but a deep, cerebral understanding of how to manipulate the machinery of the state. He isn’t a blind ideologue; he is a technocrat who uses identity politics as a surgical tool. His "Mama" persona is the soft mask for a man obsessed with being "overprepared," a trait that makes him exceptionally efficient in governance but terrifyingly efficient in silencing dissent. There is a cold, calculated pragmatism in how he balances bridge-building with bridge-burning. He doesn't hate his enemies so much as he views them as obstacles to be liquidated or assets to be acquired. In his mind, he is the only one smart enough to navigate the chaos of the Northeast, and that belief fuels a sense of destiny that justifies any means to his end. He is the ultimate political Darwinist—he survives not because he is the strongest, but because he is the most adaptable and maybe he is right or so the BJP supporters believe in.

He has seen the inside of every room, played every side of the board, and he seems to have excellent grasp of current political situation in India and his state. The wind might change again, and if it does, don't be surprised to see him reinventing himself once more—always at the top. Although question remains on what may come in these state elections in Assam but if you were to ask me he seems to be pretty confident in getting back into state assembly comfortably yet again. Then again don't just believe me, keep an eye out for the results. It holds very much importance in politics of North-East Indian politics.&#x20;
