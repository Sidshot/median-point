> There is something inherently diabolical about a man who can shed his political skin as easily as a snake, only to emerge more deadly than before. The transformation of Himanta Biswa Sarma—from an ardent, hardline Congress loyalist to the poster child of the BJP’s right-wing politics—isn’t just a change of heart; it is a meticulously engineered masterclass in opportunism.

As I write this piece late at night in my room, the political accusations against Sarma have become rather intense. The latest storm to hit this fortress of power comes from Pawan Khera, who recently threw a bucket of mud directly at the Chief Minister’s doorstep, alleging a global web of hidden assets. [The accusations are wild](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/assam-police-at-congress-leader-pawan-kheras-residence-in-delhi/article70833431.ece)—multiple foreign passports from the UAE to Antigua, luxury properties in Dubai, and secret investments in Wyoming. It is the kind of high-stakes scandal that would bury a lesser politician, but Sarma’s response was characteristically aggressive. He did not just deny it; he weaponized the attack, claiming the documents were “Photoshopped” fakes sourced from Pakistani social media to rig the upcoming elections. By filing FIRs and threatening to drag the top Congress leadership into the legal mire, he turned a corruption allegation into a battle over national security and “foreign-influenced misinformation.” It is a classic Himanta move: do not just defend the goal, burn down the other team’s stadium.

But this instinct—to escalate, to reframe, to dominate the narrative—is not new. It is the product of a long political apprenticeship where survival depended not on purity of thought, but on speed of adaptation. Sarma does not wait for storms to pass; he steps into them, redirects them, and ensures that by the time the dust settles, the conversation is no longer about the allegation but about the enemy who raised it.

Let me give you a bit of his past, which is quite a journey in itself. His career did not begin in a plush office; it began in the cut-throat trenches of student politics at Cotton College, where he learned early that loyalty is a currency meant to be spent, not saved. He cut his teeth with the AASU, only to later turn around and dismantle his own mentors, like Bhrigu Phukan, the moment a better offer from Congress came along. For over a decade, he was the ultimate fixer for Tarun Gogoi, building a reputation for ruthless efficiency—medical colleges, teacher appointments, infrastructure—while quietly constructing a parallel power structure that eventually outgrew the man who gave him a seat at the table.

> #### **The Foundation: Student Power and Early Betrayals (1980s – 1990s) /**
>
> * **Late 1980s:** A 18-year-old student at Cotton College, Sarma makes his mark in the All Assam Students' Union (AASU). He is elected Assistant General Secretary in 1987 and General Secretary in 1988, showing an early knack for "political acumen" and strategy while others were still focused on textbooks.
> * **Early 1990s:** He serves as the General Secretary of the AASU-affiliated All Guwahati Students' Union. However, the first signs of his "sniffing the wind" appear when he shifts his gaze toward the Congress party, realizing that student activism has its limits.
> * **1996:** He makes his first attempt at the Assembly elections on a Congress ticket from Jalukbari at the age of 27. He loses to the AGP veteran Bhrigu Phukan—the very man he would eventually dismantle.
>
> #### **The Fixer: The Congress Era (2001 – 2014)**
>
> * **2001:** Sarma wins the Jalukbari seat, a fortress he has never lost since. He quickly becomes the "blue-eyed boy" of Chief Minister Tarun Gogoi.
> * **2002:** Appointed as a junior minister in Gogoi's cabinet. He begins building a reputation for "ruthless efficiency," handling heavy portfolios like Health, Education, and Finance.
> * **2006 – 2011:** He becomes the ultimate "fixer" for the Congress, credited with the party’s victory in the 2011 elections. He was known to stay at the office until 2:00 AM, mastering every file, budget, and bureaucratic loophole. He wasn't just a politician; he was a technocrat of power.
> * **2012 – 2014:** The "diabolical" shift begins. Sensing that the Congress "family" business (the Gandhis) would never let him lead over Gogoi’s son, Gaurav, he begins a quiet rebellion. He realizes the Congress ship is sinking and starts looking for a new boat.
>
> #### **The Transformation: The Saffron Shift (2015 – 2021)**
>
> * **2015:** In a move that shocked the old guard, he joins the BJP. He doesn't just join; he brings a parallel power structure with him, effectively hollowing out the Congress in Assam.
> * **2016:** He acts as the chief architect for the first-ever BJP government in the state. While Sarbananda Sonowal becomes CM, Sarma is the "Number 2" who actually runs the machinery, handling the COVID-19 pandemic and the anti-CAA protests with a mix of "Mama" charm and iron-fisted governance.
> * **2021:** The meticulous plan reaches its climax. After another electoral win, he finally pushes aside the "mild-mannered" Sonowal to take the throne. He is sworn in as the 15th Chief Minister of Assam.
>
> #### **The Sovereign: Absolute Authority (2022 – Present)**
>
> * **2022 – 2024:** He evolves into a "Right-wing hardcore nationalist," using identity politics as a surgical tool. He weaponizes the law against dissenters, files FIRs against national critics, and maintains a "PhD in power" by being the most aggressive defender of his new ideology.
> * **2025 – 2026:** Facing accusations from the likes of Pawan Khera, he remains "characteristically aggressive," turning every corruption allegation into a battle of national pride. As the April 2026 elections loom, he stands as a "high-functioning survivalist," ready to reinvent himself yet again if the wind changes direction.

![](/images/blog/mama-or-a-polarising-figure-the-evolution-of-himanta-biswa-sarma/image.png)

Inside that Congress system, Sarma was not merely a minister; he was an operator. Files moved because he wanted them to move. Decisions were expedited because he understood where pressure needed to be applied. He mastered the invisible levers of governance—the bureaucracy, the networks, the informal chains of command that often matter more than formal authority. By the time his fallout with the Congress leadership became public, the reality was already clear: he had outgrown the structure that once contained him.

When the Congress family refused to let him sit at the head of the table, he did not simply leave; he burned the bridge and built a highway to the BJP. He traded the white khadi for the saffron scarf not out of sudden conviction, but because he understood that the throne he wanted was no longer in the hands of the old guard. From campus agitator to undisputed kingmaker of the Northeast, his career is a trail of outsmarted allies and discarded ideologies, all leading to the one thing he actually seems to believe in: absolute control. Machiavelli would have recognized the pattern instantly.

**Sniffing the Wind**

> This was never a sudden epiphany or a spiritual awakening. This was a man sniffing the direction of the wind and realizing his boat was on a sinking Congress ship. He did not just jump overboard; he steered himself directly into the path of the winning side. That level of calculation makes him a dangerous and unpredictable force. For a politician like Sarma, ideology is a secondary tool—a costume to be swapped out when the audience changes. His only true north is his own vested interest.

What sets Sarma apart, however, is not just that he adapts—it is how quickly and completely he adapts. Most politicians carry remnants of their past; Sarma erases his. The transition from Congress secularism to BJP’s identity-driven politics was not gradual—it was surgical. Within a short span, he was not just aligned with the BJP; he was one of its most aggressive and articulate voices in the Northeast, often outpacing even long-time ideological loyalists. Sarma’s real talent lies in his ability to shape perception as much as policy. He does not just govern; he constructs a narrative around governance. Welfare schemes, infrastructure pushes, administrative efficiency—these are real and visible, and they give his supporters tangible reasons to stay loyal. Roads get built, institutions get expanded, and government machinery often moves with unusual speed under his watch. These are not illusions; they are measurable outcomes that anchor his credibility. But layered over this is a carefully engineered persona: the accessible “Mama,” the sharp debater, the man who appears always in control. He speaks in a language that resonates locally, yet operates with a strategic clarity that is national in scope. The result is a political figure whose image often travels faster than the reality it is built on. This duality—delivery on the ground, dominance in narrative—is what makes him difficult to counter. Critics often focus on his rhetoric, his ideological shifts, or the controversies surrounding him. Supporters, on the other hand, point to visible governance outcomes and dismiss the rest as noise. Between these two realities lies the actual space in which Sarma operates—and thrives.

This is where the gap becomes important. For a large section of his base, outcomes matter—but perception matters more. Delivery of schemes and visible development create a baseline of credibility; identity politics and controlled messaging amplify it into unwavering support. Allegations, no matter how serious, struggle to penetrate this shield because they are quickly reframed as attacks on identity, region, or nation itself. In that sense, Sarma does not merely survive controversies—he absorbs them and converts them into political capital.

There is also a deeper structural advantage at play. In a region like the Northeast, where politics is often fragmented, layered with ethnic complexities and historical grievances, a leader who can project decisiveness gains disproportionate traction. Sarma understands this terrain intimately. He does not attempt to simplify it; he navigates it. He builds alliances where necessary, applies pressure where useful, and ensures that he remains central to every equation. Even his critics are often forced to engage on terms that he sets.

Ultimately, we are looking at a man for whom the cost of power is never too high. He is loathsome to those who value consistency, but he is undeniably effective at staying relevant. By projecting himself as the “protector” of identity while simultaneously weaponizing fear, he has built a fortress of support that feels less like a democratic mandate and more like a captured audience. There is a cold, calculated pragmatism in the way he balances bridge-building with bridge-burning. He does not hate his enemies so much as he views them as obstacles to be eliminated or assets to be acquired. In his mind, he is the only one smart enough to navigate the chaos of the Northeast, and that belief fuels a sense of destiny that justifies almost any means to his end. He is the ultimate political Darwinist—he survives not because he is the strongest, but because he is the most adaptable. And perhaps that, more than ideology, is the real source of his power.

And yet, there remains an unresolved question beneath all of this: is Sarma shaping the system, or is he merely the most refined product of it? Because if a political ecosystem consistently rewards adaptability over consistency, narrative over ideology, and control over consensus, then Sarma is not an exception—he is the logical outcome. Remove him, and the system will produce another version, perhaps less efficient, perhaps less sharp, but driven by the same incentives.

&#x20;The conclusion is simple: the Congress party considers him a “gaddar,” while the BJP and its hardcore supporters see him as someone who has returned home. Psychologically, Sarma is a high-functioning survivalist who treats politics like a blood sport played with grandmaster precision. He possesses a PhD in power—not just the literal degree on his wall, but a deep, cerebral understanding of how to manipulate the machinery of the state. He is not a blind ideologue; he is a technocrat who uses identity politics as a surgical tool. His “Mama” persona is the soft mask of a man obsessed with being overprepared, a trait that makes him exceptionally efficient in governance, but equally efficient in controlling narrative - a poltical master at work. He has seen the inside of every room, played every side of the board, and seems to have an excellent grasp of the current political situation in India and in his state. The wind might change again, and if it does, do not be surprised to see him reinventing himself once more—always at the top. The question remains what comes next in the Assam state elections, but if you ask me, he looks confident of returning to the Assembly comfortably once again. Then again, do not just take my word for it. Keep an eye on the results. It matters deeply in the politics of Northeast India.
